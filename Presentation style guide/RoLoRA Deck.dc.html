LoRA trains a low-rank update, not the full model

The pretrained weight \(W_0\) stays frozen. Fine-tuning learns two small matrices whose product becomes the task-specific update.
Adapted weight
\[W = W_0 + \alpha AB\]
The two factors are low-rank: \(A\) is tall and narrow \((d\times r)\) and \(B\) is short and wide \((r\times d)\). Only they move during training; \(W_0\) is reused by every client.
\(A\)
\(d\times r\) · tall & narrow
×
\(B\)
\(r\times d\) · short & wide
Small payload
Send \(A+B\), not all of \(W\).
Federated catch
The server aggregates factors, not products.

LoRA cuts communication but creates an aggregation problem

The server wants to average each client's effective update. FedAvg-LoRA averages factors separately, which creates mixed client products.
What the server should average
\[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} A_i B_i\]
Every product \(A_iB_i\) belongs to one trained client model.
What FedAvg-LoRA computes
\[\Big(\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_i A_i\Big)\Big(\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_i B_i\Big)=\bar A\,\bar B\]
This introduces terms like \(A_1B_2\): one client's basis with another client's coefficients.
The gap is small when clients look alike, but grows under non-IID data and many clients.

RoLoRA makes each aggregation step exact

B-PHASE
All clients share \(A^t\), freeze it, update \(B_i\).
\[\frac{1}{N}\sum_i A^tB_i^{t+1}=A^t\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_i B_i^{t+1}\Big)\]
A-PHASE
All clients share \(B^{t+1}\), freeze it, update \(A_i\).
\[\frac{1}{N}\sum_i A_i^{t+1}B^{t+1}=\Big(\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_i A_i^{t+1}\Big)B^{t+1}\]
B→ A → B → A
One shared frozen factor can be factored out of the client average.

Three complementary ideas for improving RoLoRA

1
Condition the shared basis
  • Toy: orthogonal init of \(A\), \(\;A^\top A = I_r\).
  • RoBERTa: SVD-compensated init from \(W_0\).
  • A better-conditioned subspace reduces early client divergence.
2
Treat the two phases unequally
  • Step size \(\lambda=\tfrac{\eta_B}{\eta_A}>1\) (LoRA+).
  • BBA schedule \(B,B,A,\dots\), or adaptive refresh of \(A\) when \(B\) stalls.
  • Once \(A\) is stable, extra \(B\)-updates can fit better coefficients.
3
Limit local drift
  • Add a FedProx proximal term on the active factor.
  • It penalizes drift of the local factor from the broadcast one.
  • Keeping local factors near the broadcast factor may improve stability.

On the toy model, only conditioning clearly helps

Orthogonal \(A\) improves mean accuracy from 84.0% to 87.2% and narrows the spread.
No gain

When this toy run finishes, the story is narrow: the basis matters; extra constraints do not.

QNLI extensions are promising, but not finished

Figure 4 · QNLI extensions vs RoLoRA (50 clients, RoBERTa-base)
Critical qualification
Not significant
The report treats the BBA result as unconfirmed:
  • all arms now have 3 runs.
  • 95% intervals overlap RoLoRA;
  • clearest signal is lower run-to-run variance.

The extensions only help when they target what RoLoRA leaves open

Where there is room to move
  • Basis quality: the toy model has no pretrained \(W_0\), so orthogonal \(A\) gives a better starting subspace.
  • Frequency: once \(A\) is stable, extra \(B\)-rounds can fit coefficients without constantly moving the basis.
Where add-ons miss the failure mode
  • SVD init alone: energetic \(W_0\) directions need not be the QNLI-useful directions.
  • LoRA+: it fixes simultaneous factor training, but RoLoRA separates the phases.
  • FedProx: it dampens local drift, while RoLoRA already restricts and syncs each round.
RoLoRA reproduces, but the QNLI proxy is learning-rate and round-budget sensitive; the extension signal is lower variance more than a significant mean gain.

Conclusions

Supported by our experiments
  • RoLoRA's qualitative robustness reproduces on the toy model and QNLI proxy.
  • The advantage is clearest under heterogeneous clients.
  • Orthogonal \(A\) gives a clear toy-model gain.
  • LoRA+ step-size asymmetry and FedProx do not help in our toy screen.
  • Basis conditioning plus \(B\)-prioritized frequency is promising but not significant; lower variance is the clearest signal.
Scope limitations
  • One GLUE task, not five.
  • One client count (50).
  • RoBERTa-base, not large.
  • 20 rounds, not 30; 3 LRs, not 8.
  • 4-hour limit on DelftBlue's a100-small.
  • Single task, reduced scale, few seeds; needs more GLUE tasks, larger backbones, and more seeds.
The language-model experiments validate trends — they should not be read as a full reproduction of the original experimental grid.
